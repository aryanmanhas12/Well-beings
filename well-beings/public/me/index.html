NeuroBioPsych

The waiting room is full. So build the front door.

I'm Aryan Manhas. I build free, private, evidence-based mental-health tools — a screener that finds people early, and a system that helps them keep going afterwards. No accounts, no servers, no data leaving the device. Every claim carries a citation.

The Thesis

One mind, three layers — treat all of them.

Mental health doesn't live in one place, which is why single-layer care keeps missing. NeuroBioPsych is the commitment to work across all three at once.

Neuro

The circuits

How attention, mood and stress systems actually behave — and how habits, sleep and intervention reshape them over weeks rather than moments.

Bio

The body

Sleep timing, movement, light, physiology. These carry some of the strongest evidence in the entire field, and they are the levers a person can actually pull without a prescription.

Psych

The person and their world

Validated screeners, CBT-family techniques, and the lived context — money, family, exams, isolation — that decides whether any of it survives contact with a real week.

Software will never replace clinicians, and nothing here pretends to. But in a country with 0.75 psychiatrists per 100,000 people, the binding constraint isn't knowledge — it's reach. Evidence-first tools can find people earlier and hold them steadier than a waiting list can, and they can do it privately, for free, at the scale the gap actually demands.

The Work

Two halves of one problem

Finding people earlier, and giving them something to do next. The screener is the front door; Well-Beings is the room behind it.

Psych Screener

Live

A free, private, multilingual mental-health screening tool — built for India, usable anywhere. Validated instruments (PHQ-9, GAD-7, PHQ-4, AUDIT-C) in six languages, a guided conversation for people who don't know where to start, crisis lines for 130+ countries, and score history you keep. Alongside it: an evidence register, an ethics charter, and a manifesto on the treatment gap.

Well-Beings

Live

Screening tells you where you stand; it doesn't tell you what to do on Tuesday. Well-Beings is a five-minute chat-style check-in built on the same clinical screeners — PHQ-2→9, GAD-2→7, sleep and burnout — that turns the result into a working system: a time-blocked day, evidence-cited intervention cards, a habit stack that forgives missed days, a weekly recovery quota and a burnout radar. Every practice states its effect in plain English, not statistical notation.

How they fit

Screen early, then act early

The screener is deliberately the shallower, wider net — one visit, no commitment, six languages, pointed straight at people who may never have named what they're feeling. Well-Beings is the deeper, narrower follow-through for someone who has a result and wants a routine that accounts for it. Both run entirely in the browser, both cite their sources, and neither ever asks who you are.

Where This Goes

Goals & aspirations

Stated plainly, so they can be held against results.

  1. Grow NeuroBioPsych from a thesis into a body of work

    Research, writing and software that treat the neural, biological and psychosocial layers as one problem instead of three specialities that rarely talk.

  2. Close a measurable piece of India's treatment gap

    Tools that are free, private and in the languages people actually think in — reaching them years before a clinic could, in a system with 0.75 psychiatrists per 100,000.

  3. Keep every claim falsifiable

    No wellness fluff. Every practice cites its meta-analysis, RCT or cohort — and says so out loud when the evidence is young, mixed, or weaker than the marketing around it.

  4. Study this properly

    Take the work into formal training in psychiatry and neuroscience, where the questions get harder and the standards get stricter.

  5. Find the others

    Researchers, clinicians, designers and developers who want to work on minds — seriously, and together. Most of what's missing here is collaborators, not ideas.
